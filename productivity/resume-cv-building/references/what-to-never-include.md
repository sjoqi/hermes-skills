# Recruiter-Backed "Never Include" Guidelines

Source: Reddit r/ResumeCoverLetterTips — *"As a recruiter who reviews resumes every single day… Here are 5 things I'd never include on my resume"* (u/bored-recruiter, ~358 upvotes, 7mo ago). Plus the top-voted comment nuance. Use this when deciding what to **cut** from a resume, not just what to add.

## The 5 things a working recruiter never includes
1. **Objectives / career-goal statements** — nobody reads them. The reader wants to know if you can do *this* job, not your dreams. Pure space waste. (Note: a tight 2–3 line *Summary* that frames fit is different from an "Objective" — summary stays, objective goes.)
2. **Long buzzword self-descriptions** — "Highly motivated, detail-oriented team player with excellent communication skills…" says nothing; every resume says it, so it gets skimmed past.
3. **A photo** — not "unprofessional," but unnecessary, introduces bias, and some companies have strict no-photo policies that reject candidates outright. **North America convention: no photo. Europe: photo is often expected** — follow the target region.
4. **Lists of soft skills** (teamwork / leadership / communication as a bare bullet list) — everyone claims them; they don't differentiate. Let experience and results *prove* them instead.
5. **Bullets that just restate the job description** — "Answered calls." "Handled customer issues." Expected. What did you do *well*? What improved *because you were there*?

## Community-added 6th item
- **Skill graphs / charts with no context** (e.g. a "Skills" bar chart) — cringe; the impact should already live in the experience bullets.

## Nuance from the comments (important — don't over-apply)
- **ATS tension is real but the fix isn't padding.** Many applicants add buzzwords/objectives/soft-skill lists purely to pass ATS keyword scanners. Recruiters' counter: weave the keywords *naturally into* experience bullets, don't bolt on a separate buzzword section. The goal is one document that satisfies both human and scanner.
- **Never fabricate metrics.** Several commenters admitted inventing stats ("decreased costs by 15%") just to pass screens; recruiters say made-up numbers are often detectable and only survive if bluffed in interview. This directly supports the skill's honest-framing rule. **If you have no metric, say so and we find a truthful proxy** (volume, scope, users, tools, a qualitative outcome) — or we leave the number off.
- **The 5-second test** (u/topCSjobs): *"If you can't back a line up in a few seconds, delete it."* Apply this to every bullet.
- **LinkedIn photo paradox:** recruiters only open your LinkedIn *after* already deciding to interview from the resume — so the resume itself stays photo-free regardless.
- **No-metrics / dead-end roles:** some applicants (regulated, gov, entry-level) genuinely can't quantify impact. That's exactly the case for a strong `Projects & Self-Directed Work` section (see honest-framing rule) rather than inventing numbers.

## How to use this in the build
- Run every draft bullet through: (a) does it restate the JD? cut/rewrite. (b) is it a bare soft-skill claim? cut or attach a proof. (c) can it survive the 5-second-backup test? if not, cut.
- Keep `Summary` (fit-framing) but delete any "Objective / career goals" line.
- No photo on NA-targeted resumes. Flag photo only when the target region expects it.
